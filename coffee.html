Bukalasi coffee, grown by women who own their land.

Our flagship coffee grows in the village of Bukalasi, Bududa District, high on Mt. Elgon in Eastern Uganda.

The coffee facts.

From our master roaster’s notes on Uganda Bududa Bukalasi.

Species
Coffea arabica
Process
Fully washed
Altitude
1,750 to 1,860 m
Body
Medium, like fruit juice in the mouth
Flavour
Round, not sharp. Dried stone fruit, like dried cherries or cranberries. A sweet, earthy walnut note.
Finish
Floral and herbal, like lavender or sweet basil. Sometimes a light spice, like allspice.
Added flavour
None. The notes come from the bean.

Organic, without the label.

The farmers use no synthetic fertilizer or pesticide. They make fertilizer from coffee cherry pulp and manure, and a plant spray from Mexican sunflower, peri-peri chili and ashes.

The trees grow in shade, between local plants. The farmers keep bees and let animals graze. Water comes from springs, waterfalls and rain tanks. Any electricity on the farms is solar.

We are not certified organic. We chose the B Corp check instead, because it looks at farming, pay, energy and water together.

Better than fair trade.

We buy directly from the farmer groups and pay above the Fair Trade floor price. When we started, we offered 25 to 30% more than other buyers.

250+farmers in Bukalasi worked with Endiro Growers in 2021.
2015The year the first farmer group started, about 200 women.

No light, medium or dark.

Colour and oil only tell part of the story. We roast each bean for the profile its origin can give. For Bukalasi that is a medium body, mild acidity and a sweet, fruity finish.

How we brew it in the cafés

Espresso

House coffee

Brewed coffee, the everyday cup.

Primula pour over

A flat-bottomed steel filter for a clean cup.

Aeropress

French press

Where it grows.

Bukalasi is in Bududa District, on the slopes of Mt. Elgon near the Kenya border. We also work with growers near Sipi Falls in Kapchorwa, where our Lacam Lodge café looks over the valley.
